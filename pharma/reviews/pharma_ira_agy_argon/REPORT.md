## 1. Verdict: **PASS WITH FIXES**

All core drug-level numbers for the ten Initial Price Applicability Year (IPAY) 2026 selected drugs replicate **to the exact decimal place** across all three empirical angles: (a) Medicare Part D gross spending per claim (`2025 Q1-Q4` vs. `2026 Q1`), (b) wholesale acquisition cost (WAC) adjustments in NADAC (`2024–2026`), and (c) retail pharmacy acquisition cost (`NADAC`) relative to the `2026` Maximum Fair Price (`MFP`) per unit (`2025-12-31` vs. `2026-10-21`). The paper's statutory explanation of how MFPs enter Part D point-of-sale prices and how retrospective Medicare Transaction Facilitator (MTF) refunds create an invoice-to-MFP gap is accurate and appropriately cautious about net vs. gross spending.

However, **six concrete empirical and interpretive defects** in `pharma/scripts/ira_analysis.py`, `pharma/outputs/ira/summary.md`, and `pharma/ira_findings.md` require correction:
1. **Comparator Contamination (`43.9%` of "Other brands" claims are hyphenated multi-source generics) and Cross-Table Inconsistency**: A regex defect in `partd_per_claim()` (`r"^([A-Z0-9-]+)"`) includes hyphens in the "first word" check, misclassifying **95 multi-ingredient generic rows** (`Hydrocodone-Acetaminophen`, `Oxycodone-Acetaminophen`, `Amoxicillin-Clavulanate Potass`, `Losartan-Hydrochlorothiazide`, etc., totaling **`89.1 million` claims or `43.9%` of all 2025 claims** in the `831` `"Other brands"`) as brands, while `selected_ingredient()` uses unanchored substring matching that drops **non-selected brand combinations** (`Synjardy`, `Synjardy XR`, `Glyxambi`, `Trijardy XR`, `Xigduo XR`, `Jentadueto`) and `Rhapsido` (`Remibrutinib`, matched via `"IBRUTINIB"` substring) that are kept in `"Other brands"` in Table 1.
2. **Misattribution of the `+9.5%` (`Other brands`) and `+13.2%` (`IPAY 2028`) Pooled Spending-per-Claim Increases to Seasonality**: `ira_findings.md` suggests the `+9.5%` pooled per-claim increase in `"Other brands"` reflects Q1 seasonality. A Laspeyres decomposition holding 2025 claim shares fixed shows the within-drug claim-weighted change is only **`+1.24%`** for `"Other brands"` (`+1.23%` for IPAY 2028, `+1.48%` for IPAY 2027); **81% (`+7.67 pp`) of the `+9.47%` pooled rise** in `"Other brands"` and **91% (`+12.03 pp`) of the `+13.22%` rise** in `IPAY 2028` are between-drug composition shifts toward rapidly expanding high-cost specialty/GLP-1 drugs (`Mounjaro`, `Jascayd`, `Nubeqa`, `Dupixent Pen`, `Vyvgart Hytrulo`).
3. **Truncation of `Xarelto`'s `2026-02-03` (`+2.30%`) WAC Increase and Eli Lilly's `2026-02-01` (`-70.0%`) Insulin Cuts by the `Jan 31` Cutoff**: Because `wac_january()` ends at `Jan 31`, `Xarelto`'s 19 brand NDCs (whose annual WAC increase occurred on `2024-01-24` [`+5.0%`], `2025-01-28` [`+5.0%`], and `2026-02-03` [`+2.3%`]) are counted as WAC increases in 2024 and 2025 but excluded in 2026, understating IPAY 2026's early-2026 WAC-increase share as **`18.8%` (`13/69`)** instead of **`46.4%` (`32/69` across 4 of the 9 drugs in NADAC)**.
4. **Unsupported Claim in Finding 3 that `Xarelto`, `Entresto`, `Stelara`, and `Enbrel` NADAC/MFP Ratios Were "Essentially Unchanged"**: All four drugs actively **increased** WAC by `+2.3%` to `+5.0%` in January/February 2026, raising their NADAC/MFP ratios from `2.78 -> 2.84`, `2.16 -> 2.22`, `3.16 -> 3.32`, and `3.40 -> 3.57` (only `Januvia` at `2.70 -> 2.70` was unchanged).
5. **Mislabeling `Fiasp` as `NOVOLOG` in `summary.md` Table 1 and Omitting That `30 of 33` (`90.9%`) "Other Brands" Jan 2026 WAC Cuts Are Boehringer Ingelheim Co-Formulations of `Jardiance`**: `summary.md` Table 1 labels the 2026 `-75.0%` cut on 4 `Fiasp` NDCs as `NOVOLOG` (making `NOVOLOG` appear to cut 75% in both 2024 and 2026), and does not disclose that 30 of the 33 `"Other brands"` NDCs with `>=20%` cuts in January 2026 are `Synjardy`, `Synjardy XR`, `Glyxambi`, and `Trijardy XR`, cut by the **exact same `-44.39%` on `2026-01-01`** as `Jardiance`.
6. **Unaddressed `Entresto` Days-Supply Compression (`-33.6%` units/claim) and Expired `Entresto Sprinkle` `01/01/2026` MFPs (`9.46x` ratio)**: Generic `Sacubitril-Valsartan` entry (`1.08M` Q1 claims) reduced brand `Entresto` annualized claims by `86.5%` and compressed implied units per claim from `93.4` (`2024`) to `62.0` (`2026 Q1`), inflating its per-claim drop (`-70.0%`) relative to its per-unit price drop (`-54.7%`). In NADAC, filtering strictly to `MFP Effective Date == "01/01/2026"` applies an expired `$1.229167/EA` MFP (end-dated `05/31/2026`) to the two `Entresto Sprinkle` NDCs after CMS updated their MFPs to `$5.52–$5.65/EA` on `06/01/2026`.

## 3. Part 3 Comparison: Disagreements and Unsupported Statements in `summary.md` and `ira_findings.md`

1. **Contamination of the `831` `"Other brands"` Part D Comparator by `95` Hyphenated Multi-Source Generics (`43.9%` of Claims) and Simultaneous Exclusion of Non-Selected Brand Combinations**:
   - In `pharma/scripts/ira_analysis.py` (lines 166–169), `"Other brands"` filters out generic-named rows using `first = q.B.str.extract(r"^([A-Z0-9-]+)", expand=False)` and checks whether `first` appears in `g.replace("/", " ").replace(",", " ").split()`. Because CMS Part D formats multi-ingredient generic `Brnd_Name` strings with hyphens (`HYDROCODONE-ACETAMINOPHEN`) and `Gnrc_Name` strings with slashes (`HYDROCODONE/ACETAMINOPHEN`), including `-` in the regex causes `first` to retain the hyphen (`"HYDROCODONE-ACETAMINOPHEN"`), which never matches the slash-split tokens (`["HYDROCODONE", "ACETAMINOPHEN"]`).
   - As a result, **95 multi-ingredient generic products** (`Hydrocodone-Acetaminophen` [`20.1M` claims, 19 manufacturers], `Oxycodone-Acetaminophen` [`9.0M` claims, 15 manufacturers], `Amoxicillin-Clavulanate Potass` [`8.3M` claims, 14 manufacturers], `Losartan-Hydrochlorothiazide` [`5.8M` claims, 9 manufacturers], `Lisinopril-Hydrochlorothiazide` [`5.4M` claims, 8 manufacturers], etc.) are misclassified into `"Other brands"`. These 95 generic rows account for **`89,067,118` of the `202,683,062` claims (`43.9%`)** in `"Other brands"`, contradicting the statement in `pharma/ira_findings.md` (line 37) that `"Other brands" ... still contains a few generic products`. (In addition, `Insulin Syringe` with truncated `Gnrc_Name == "Syring-Needl,Disp,Insul,0.3 Ml"` bypasses `SUPPLY`, and non-hyphenated generics like `Vitamin D2` [`1.68M` claims, 8 manufacturers] and `Sodium Fluoride` remain.)
   - Conversely, `selected_ingredient(g)` (lines 152–157) checks `any(all(c in g for c in cs) for cs in comps)`, which drops any multi-ingredient combination drug containing a selected drug's active moiety (`Synjardy`, `Synjardy XR`, `Glyxambi`, `Trijardy XR`, `Xigduo XR`, `Jentadueto`, `Jentadueto XR`, `Novolog Mix 70-30`) as well as `Rhapsido` (`Remibrutinib`, because `"IBRUTINIB"` is a substring of `"REMIBRUTINIB"`). Because `Synjardy` (`-42.7%`), `Synjardy XR` (`-42.6%`), `Glyxambi` (`-43.4%`), and `Trijardy XR` (`-44.0%`) all fell by `>42%` in Part D after Boehringer Ingelheim's `2026-01-01` WAC cut, excluding them from Table 3 while keeping them in `"Other brands"` in Table 1 is internally inconsistent.
2. **Unsupported Attribution of the `+9.5%` (`Other brands`) and `+13.2%` (`IPAY 2028`) Pooled Per-Claim Changes to Seasonality (`pharma/ira_findings.md`, line 36)**:
   - `ira_findings.md` states: *"The near-zero median for other brands does not rule out seasonality (their pooled per-claim spending rose 9.5%)."*
   - Decomposing `pooled_pct_change` into a fixed-weight Laspeyres index (holding 2025 claim shares constant) vs. between-drug claim-share shifts proves that the within-drug claim-weighted change is only **`+1.24%`** for `"Other brands"`, **`+1.23%`** for `IPAY 2028`, and **`+1.48%`** for `IPAY 2027`. Between-drug claim-share shifts alone (holding every drug's spending per claim at its 2025 level) raise the pooled average by **`+7.67%`** in `"Other brands"` (**81% of the `+9.47%` rise**, driven by rapid secular growth in high-cost drugs like `Mounjaro` [`+22%` annualized claims at `$1,325/claim`], `Jascayd` [`+1,537%` at `$16,723/claim`], `Nubeqa` [`+38%` at `$13,227/claim`], `Dupixent Pen` [`+20%` at `$4,296/claim`], and `Vyvgart Hytrulo` [`+80%` at `$60,382/claim`]) and by **`+12.03%`** in `IPAY 2028` (**91% of the `+13.22%` rise**, driven by a `-25.2%` claim drop in low-cost `Anoro Ellipta` [`$619/claim`]).
3. **Truncation of `Xarelto`'s `2026-02-03` (`+2.30%`) WAC Increase by the `Jan 31` Window Cutoff (`summary.md` Table 1; `ira_findings.md` Finding 2)**:
   - `wac_january()` restricts WAC events to `Effective Date` between `Dec 20` and `Jan 31`. For `Xarelto` (`19` brand NDCs, or `27.5%` of all `69` IPAY 2026 brand NDCs), Janssen's annual WAC increase took effect on `2024-01-24` (`+5.0%`), `2025-01-28` (`+5.0%`), and **`2026-02-03` (`+2.3%`)**. Missing the `Jan 31` cutoff by 3 days causes Table 1 and Finding 2 to report that only **`13` (`18.8%`)** of IPAY 2026 brand NDCs had a WAC increase in 2026, when in fact **`32` (`46.4%`)** of the 69 IPAY 2026 brand NDCs (4 of the 9 drugs in NADAC: `Enbrel`, `Entresto`, `Stelara`, and `Xarelto`) increased WAC in early 2026. Extending the window by 3 days also captures Eli Lilly's `2026-02-01` `-70.0%` WAC cut on 5 `Lyumjev` and `Humalog 200 U/mL` brand NDCs.
4. **Unsupported Statement in Finding 3 That `Xarelto (2.84), Entresto (2.22), Stelara (3.32) and Enbrel (3.57)` Were "Essentially Unchanged" (`pharma/ira_findings.md`, line 27)**:
   - While `Januvia` was unchanged (`2.7008 -> 2.6986`), `Enbrel` (`3.40 -> 3.57`, `+5.1%`), `Stelara` (`3.16 -> 3.32`, `+5.1%`), `Entresto` (`2.16 -> 2.22`, `+2.9%`), and `Xarelto` (`2.78 -> 2.84`, `+2.2%`) all **increased** their NADAC-to-MFP ratios between December 2025 and October 2026 because all four manufacturers raised their WAC list prices by `+2.3%` to `+5.0%` in January/February 2026.
5. **Mislabeling `Fiasp`'s 2026 WAC Cut as `NOVOLOG` in `summary.md` Table 1 and Omitting Non-Independence of the `33` `"Other brands"` WAC Cuts**:
   - In `summary.md` Table 1 ("Cuts on selected drugs (all Januaries)"), both the `2024-01-01` cut (`3` NovoLog NDCs) and the `2026-01-01` cut (`4` Fiasp NDCs) are labeled `NOVOLOG`.
   - Furthermore, in Table 1 (`2026 | Other brands | 33 NDCs | 1.5%`), **`30` of the `33` NDCs (`90.9%`)** belong to Boehringer Ingelheim's non-selected empagliflozin combinations (`Synjardy` [8], `Synjardy XR` [8], `Glyxambi` [6], `Trijardy XR` [8]), which were cut by the **exact same `-44.39%` on `2026-01-01`** as `Jardiance` (`empagliflozin`).

## 4. Ranked Methods Findings (Part 2 Analysis and Concrete Fixes)

### Finding 1 (Rank 1): Gross Part D Spending Reflects Point-of-Sale MFP Effectuation, Whereas Retail NADAC Reflects Unadjusted Wholesale Invoice Prices Due to Retrospective MTF Refunds
- **Methodological Issue**: Why did gross Part D spending per claim fall by `-39.9%` to `-70.8%` in `2026 Q1` for drugs whose WAC and NADAC **increased** in early 2026 (`Enbrel` `3.57x` MFP, `Stelara` `3.32x`, `Xarelto` `2.84x`, `Entresto` `2.22x`) or stayed flat (`Januvia` `2.70x`)?
  - Under Social Security Act Section 1860D-2(d)(1)(D) and Section 1193(a)(1)/(3) (added by Pub. L. 117-169), the Part D point-of-sale "negotiated price" reported on Prescription Drug Event (PDE) records for a selected drug cannot exceed the MFP plus dispensing fees. Using 2024 average units per claim (`Tot_Dsg_Unts_2024 / Tot_Clms_2024` from `partd_spending_annual.csv`), `Units_Per_Claim_2024 * MFP_Per_Unit` predicts `2026 Q1` gross spending per claim within normal Q1 days-supply variation across the selected drugs (e.g., `Eliquis`: `89.61 EA * $4.1855/EA = $375.06` vs. `$399.01` observed; `Enbrel`: `4.33 mL * $573.72/mL = $2,485.31` vs. `$2,536.74` observed; `Xarelto`: `54.10 EA * $6.586/EA = $356.32` vs. `$374.04` observed).
  - Conversely, under Section 40.4 of the CMS Final Guidance, manufacturers may effectuate the MFP to dispensing pharmacies either **prospectively** (lowering upfront acquisition cost) or **retrospectively** via the **Medicare Transaction Facilitator (MTF) Payment Module**, which issues a `Standard Default Refund Amount = WAC - MFP` within 14 calendar days of PDE claim verification. Because CMS's NADAC survey measures wholesale purchase invoices **before** off-invoice rebates or retrospective MTF refunds, NADAC remains at `~96%–98%` of WAC (`1.33x–3.57x` MFP for 8 of the 9 retail drugs).
- **Concrete Fix**: Add a column to `partd_per_claim_by_drug.csv` and `summary.md` comparing observed `2026 Q1` spending per claim against `UPC_2024 * MFP_Per_Unit` from `partd_spending_annual.csv`, and explicitly distinguish the 4 drugs that cut WAC in Jan 2026 (`Eliquis`, `Jardiance`, `Farxiga`, `Fiasp`), the 2 drugs that cut WAC in 2024–2025 (`NovoLog`, `Januvia`), and the 4 drugs that **raised** WAC in Jan/Feb 2026 (`Enbrel`, `Entresto`, `Stelara`, `Xarelto`) and rely entirely on retrospective MTF refunds.
- **Primary Source**: [CMS Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Final Guidance for IPAY 2027 and Manufacturer Effectuation of the MFP in 2026 and 2027 (Sections 40.4 and 90.2)](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/medicare-drug-price-negotiation-final-guidance-ipay-2027-and-manufacturer-effectuation-mfp-2026-2027.pdf) and [Pub. L. 117-169, Section 11001 (SSA §§ 1193(a) and 1860D-2(d)(1)(D))](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-117publ169/pdf/PLAW-117publ169.pdf).

### Finding 2 (Rank 2): Comparing 2025 Full-Year with 2026 Q1 Per-Claim Spending Conflates Unit Price with Days-Supply Compression and Generic/Biosimilar Volume Flight
- **Methodological Issue**: Because `partd_spending_quarterly.csv` omits `Tot_Dsg_Unts` and `Tot_Days_Supply`, `Tot_Spndng / Tot_Clms` is vulnerable to five confounding biases when comparing full-year `2025 (Q1-Q4)` against preliminary `2026 (Q1)`:
  1. **Generic and biosimilar entry inducing days-supply compression**: Between 2025 and 2026 Q1, generic `Sacubitril-Valsartan` grew to `1,076,811` Q1 claims (`$172.50/claim`), reducing brand `Entresto` annualized claims by **`-86.5%`**. Dividing `Entresto`'s 2026 Q1 spending per claim (`$323.74`) by its tablet MFP (`$5.2205/EA`) reveals that implied units per claim fell **`-33.6%`** (from `93.41` tablets/claim in 2024 to `62.01` tablets/claim in 2026 Q1) as 90-day maintenance fills migrated to generics, inflating `Entresto`'s per-claim drop to **`-70.0%`** vs. its true per-unit price drop of **`-54.7%`**. Similar volume flight hit `Stelara` (**`-43.8%`** annualized claims as 6 ustekinumab biosimilars launched) and `Farxiga` (**`-30.5%`** annualized claims as generic `Dapagliflozin` reached `746,331` Q1 claims).
  2. **Intra-family aggregation bias (`NovoLog` vs. `Fiasp`)**: Pooling `NovoLog` (`-8.8%` SPC change, `+46.2%` annualized claims after its Jan 2024 `-75%` WAC cut) with `Fiasp` (`-72.1%` SPC change after its Jan 2026 `-75%` WAC cut) produces a `-30.4%` blended change that represents neither product.
  3. **Between-drug Simpson's paradox in pooled cohort metrics**: As shown in Section 3.2, `pooled_pct_change` (`+9.5%` for Other brands, `+13.2%` for IPAY 2028) is dominated (`81%–91%`) by between-drug claim-share shifts rather than within-drug price changes (`+1.24%` and `+1.23%` using fixed 2025 claim weights).
  4. **Q1 benefit-year reset and the 2025/2026 Part D redesign**: The IRA Part D redesign eliminated the coverage gap, capped out-of-pocket spending at `$2,000` in 2025 (`$2,100` in 2026), and introduced the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan, shifting Q1 refill timing and 30-day vs. 90-day fill mix during the annual deductible reset.
  5. **Preliminary Q1 PDE lag**: CMS extracts Q1 data ~3 months after quarter-end, before late specialty/mail-order PDE adjustments settle.
- **Concrete Fix**: (i) Report a **2025-claim-weighted (Laspeyres) percentage change** (`sum(w_2025 * SPC_2026Q1) / sum(w_2025 * SPC_2025) - 1`) instead of the un-indexed `pooled_pct_change`; (ii) decompose per-claim changes using `Tot_Dsg_Unts_2024 / Tot_Clms_2024` from `partd_spending_annual.csv` and combine brand + generic/biosimilar molecule totals (`Entresto + Sacubitril-Valsartan`, `Farxiga + Dapagliflozin`, `Stelara + Ustekinumab biosimilars`) in a sensitivity table; (iii) report `NovoLog` and `Fiasp` separately; and (iv) benchmark Q1-to-FY seasonality using archived prior-year Q1 CMS Part D files.
- **Primary Source**: [CMS Medicare Part D Spending by Drug Dataset & Quarterly Methodology Documentation](https://data.cms.gov/summary-statistics-on-use-and-payments/medicare-medicaid-spending-by-drug/medicare-part-d-spending-by-drug) and [CMS Final CY 2025 Part D Redesign Program Instructions](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/cy-2025-part-d-redesign-program-instructions.pdf).

### Finding 3 (Rank 3): The 2024 Medicaid AMP Rebate Cap Removal Explains Pre-2026 and Non-Selected List-Price Cuts (`NovoLog`, `Januvia`, `Janumet`, `Linzess`, `Tresiba`, `Synjardy/Glyxambi/Trijardy XR`)
- **Methodological Issue**: Why did `Eliquis` (`-43.0%`), `Jardiance` (`-44.4%`), `Farxiga` (`-37.0%`), and `Fiasp` (`-75.0%`) cut WAC on `2026-01-01`, while `NovoLog` (`-75.0%` on `2024-01-01`), `Januvia` (`-42.4%` on `2025-01-01`), IPAY 2027 `Janumet` (`-42.4%` on `2025-01-01`), IPAY 2027 `Linzess` (`-50.3%` on `2026-01-01`), and non-selected insulins/combinations (`Tresiba` `-72.2%`, `Lyumjev` `-70.0%`, `Synjardy / Synjardy XR / Glyxambi / Trijardy XR` `-44.39%`) also took large WAC cuts when no 2026 MFP applied to them?
  - **Medicaid 100%-of-AMP rebate cap removal (effective `2024-01-01`)**: Section 9816 of the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021 repealed the statutory cap (SSA § 1927(c)(2)(D)) that limited Medicaid Unit Rebate Amounts (URAs) to 100% of Average Manufacturer Price (AMP). For mature drugs with large cumulative CPI-U inflation penalties (`NovoLog`, `Fiasp`, `Tresiba`, `Humalog`, `Lyumjev`, `Januvia`, `Janumet`, `Linzess`), uncapped URAs exceeded 100% of AMP ("penny pricing" or negative net Medicaid revenue, where manufacturers owed state Medicaid programs more than the drug's price). Cutting WAC/AMP by 40%–78% extinguishes the excess inflation penalty and lowers statutory **340B ceiling prices** (`AMP - URA`) back to a positive floor.
  - **Active-ingredient reference pricing and MTF working-capital avoidance**: For `Jardiance`, Boehringer Ingelheim cut non-selected `Synjardy`, `Synjardy XR`, `Glyxambi`, and `Trijardy XR` by the **exact same `-44.39%` on `2026-01-01`** to preserve fixed-dose combination pricing parity (`empagliflozin` + cheap generic `metformin`) and reduce retrospective MTF float (`WAC - MFP`) and PBM gross-to-net bubble exposure.
  - **How to distinguish these mechanisms empirically**: (1) **Timing relative to MFP effective year** (cuts in 2024/2025 or on IPAY 2027/non-selected drugs precede MFP applicability and track AMP-cap exposure); (2) **Pre-cut cumulative NADAC/WAC inflation** (drugs cutting WAC had large cumulative pre-2024 price increases triggering Medicaid inflation rebates >75% of AMP, whereas non-cutting IPAY 2026 specialty biologics like `Imbruvica` have low Medicaid volume share); and (3) **Post-cut NADAC/MFP ratio** (`Fiasp` and `NovoLog` cut to `1.00x` MFP to eliminate MTF refunds, whereas `Eliquis` [`1.33x`], `Jardiance` [`1.65x`], and `Farxiga` [`1.99x`] stopped above the MFP at the level that resets Medicaid/PBM rebate caps).
- **Concrete Fix**: Update `ira_findings.md` Finding 2 to explain the AMP rebate cap removal and 340B/PBM gross-to-net channels, and disclose that **30 of the 33 (`90.9%`)** `"Other brands"` NDCs cutting WAC in January 2026 in Table 1 are Boehringer Ingelheim's empagliflozin combinations (`Synjardy`, `Synjardy XR`, `Glyxambi`, `Trijardy XR`) cut alongside `Jardiance`.
- **Primary Source**: [American Rescue Plan Act of 2021, Pub. L. 117-2, Section 9816 (Sunset of Limit on Maximum Rebate Amount under SSA § 1927(c)(2)(D))](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-117publ2/pdf/PLAW-117publ2.pdf) and [Medicaid Drug Rebate Program Notice / Program Releases](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/prescription-drugs/medicaid-drug-rebate-program/index.html).

### Finding 4 (Rank 4): Regex and Substring Bugs in `partd_per_claim()` Misclassify `89.1M` Generic Claims into `"Other brands"` While Dropping Non-Selected Brand Combinations
- **Methodological Issue**: As documented in Section 3.1, `first = q.B.str.extract(r"^([A-Z0-9-]+)", expand=False)` in `pharma/scripts/ira_analysis.py` (line 166) includes `-`, causing 95 hyphenated multi-ingredient generic products (`89.1M` claims = `43.9%` of cohort claims) to be classified as `"Other brands"`, while `selected_ingredient(g)` (line 156) uses unanchored substring matching `any(all(c in g for c in cs) for cs in comps)` that drops non-selected combination brands (`Synjardy`, `Synjardy XR`, `Glyxambi`, `Trijardy XR`, `Xigduo XR`, `Jentadueto`) and `Rhapsido` (`Remibrutinib`).

  1. In `ira_analysis.py` line 166, split on hyphens as well as spaces (`first = q.B.str.extract(r"^([A-Z0-9]+)", expand=False)` and `g.replace("/", " ").replace(",", " ").replace("-", " ").split()`), or cross-link `Brnd_Name` against NADAC brand classifications (`Classification for Rate Setting == "B"`) and single-source status (`Tot_Mftr == 1`).

In `selected_ingredient(g)`, match exact normalized ingredient sets (`set(g_tokens) == set(cs)`) rather than substring inclusion so that non-selected multi-ingredient combinations (`Synjardy`, `Glyxambi`, `Trijardy XR`) and distinct molecules (`Remibrutinib`) are retained in `"Other brands"`, consistent with Table 1.
  3. Expand `SUPPLY` (`line 30`) to include `r"SYRING|NEEDL"` so truncated `"Syring-Needl,Disp,Insul,0.3 Ml"` is excluded.

### Finding 5 (Rank 5): IPAY 2027 and IPAY 2028 Are Imperfect Comparison Groups Due to Anticipatory Pricing (`Linzess`, `Janumet`, `Xeljanz`), Part B Selection (`IPAY 2028`), and Therapeutic Mix Skew
- **Methodological Issue**: Using IPAY 2027 (15 drugs) and IPAY 2028 (15 drugs) as comparison groups has three structural limitations:
  1. **Anticipatory and cross-molecule pricing contamination**: IPAY 2027 drugs were selected by `2025-02-01` and finished MFP negotiations in November 2025. Two IPAY 2027 drugs (`Janumet` `-42.4%` in Jan 2025, sharing `sitagliptin` with IPAY 2026 `Januvia`; `Linzess` `-50.3%` in Jan 2026, causing a `-49.0%` Part D per-claim drop) and one IPAY 2028 drug (`Xeljanz 5 mg` `-25.0%` in Jan 2026) already underwent major WAC resets prior to their MFP effective dates.
  2. **Part B selection in IPAY 2028**: Under SSA § 1192(d)(1), IPAY 2028 is the first year to include Medicare **Part B** drugs (`Botox`, `Cimzia`, `Entyvio`, `Orencia`, `Xolair`), which have small, unrepresentative retail Part D claim volumes compared to Part D oral/self-administered drugs.
  3. **Therapeutic-class and specialty-oncology skew**: IPAY 2027/2028 have a much higher share of `$13,000–$23,000/claim` oral oncology drugs (`Calquence`, `Ibrance`, `Pomalyst`, `Xtandi`, `Erleada`, `Kisqali`, `Lenvima`, `Verzenio`, plus `$13,470/claim` `Ofev`) than IPAY 2026 (`1` oncology drug, `Imbruvica`).
- **Concrete Fix**: Keep IPAY 2027 and IPAY 2028 as policy-cohort comparators, but add a **therapeutic-class-matched single-source Part D brand comparator** (`Tot_Mftr == 1`, matched within anticoagulants [`Pradaxa`, `Savaysa`], SGLT2/GLP-1/DPP-4 diabetes agents [`Mounjaro`, `Tradjenta`, `Brenzavvy`], autoimmune biologics [`Humira`, `Skyrizi`, `Rinvoq`, `Dupixent`, `Tremfya`], and oral oncology [`Brukinsa`, `Calquence`]), and report IPAY 2027 both with and without `Linzess` (`+2.37%` vs. `+2.42%` median).
- **Primary Source**: [CMS Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program: Selected Drugs for IPAY 2027 and IPAY 2028](https://www.cms.gov/inflation-reduction-act-and-medicare/medicare-drug-price-negotiation).

### Finding 6 (Rank 6): NADAC vs. CMS `NDC-9 MFP per Unit Price` Unit-Basis Verification Exposes `Entresto Sprinkle` (`9.46x` Expired-MFP Artifact), `Fiasp PumpCart` (`1.23x`), and `Farxiga` Authorized Generics (`0.03x`)
- **Methodological Issue**: `pharma/ira_findings.md` (line 38) states that *"the MFP file has no explicit unit field, so unit agreement was checked for plausibility, not per NDC."* In fact, while both NADAC and CMS `NDC-9 MFP per Unit Price` use the NCPDP billing unit standard (`EA`, `ML`, `GM`) at the 11-digit NDC level, per-NDC verification reveals three critical anomalies in `nadac_vs_mfp()`:
  1. **`Entresto Sprinkle` (`00078-1231-20`, `00078-1238-20`) unit-price correction (`9.46x` artifact)**: In `selected_drugs_mfp.csv`, CMS initially assigned `Entresto Sprinkle` an `01/01/2026` MFP of `$1.229167/EA` (dividing `$295.00` by 240 oral pellets/30d), end-dated that row on **`05/31/2026`**, and published corrected `06/01/2026` MFPs of **`$5.521349/EA`** and **`$5.648068/EA`** (~52–53 capsules/30d). Because `nadac_vs_mfp()` (line 109) filters strictly to `MFP Effective Date == "01/01/2026"`, it compares post-May 2026 NADAC (`$11.6288/EA`) against the expired `$1.229167/EA` MFP, producing a spurious **`9.46x`** ratio for both Sprinkle NDCs in `nadac_to_mfp_weekly.csv` and `last26`.
  2. **`Fiasp PumpCart` (`00169-3206-15`, `1.23x` ratio)**: CMS assigned this 1.6 mL cartridge the Vial MFP (`$6.956927/mL`), whereas Novo Nordisk priced it at the cartridge NADAC (`$8.57496/mL`), yielding `1.2326x`.
  3. **Generic-classified NDCs (`Classification for Rate Setting == "G"`) on the CMS IPAY 2026 list**: `Farxiga`'s two authorized generic `Dapagliflozin` NDCs (`66993-0456-30`, `66993-0457-30`, `Class == "G"`) dropped from `1.88x` in Dec 2025 to **`0.027x–0.030x`** (`$0.164–$0.184/EA` vs. `$6.05/EA` MFP) on `2026-06-03` when NADAC pooled them with multi-source generic dapagliflozin. Similarly, Novo Nordisk's 3 unbranded `Insulin Aspart` NDCs (`73070-0100/0102/0103`, `Class == "G"`) have MFPs (`$3.48–$4.48/mL`) equal to half of branded NovoLog's MFP but carry the same generic NADAC (`$6.95–$8.96/mL`), yielding **`2.00x`** and pulling the 10-NDC `NovoLog/Fiasp` December 2025 median down from **`3.99x`** (across the 7 brand NDCs) to **`2.00x`**.
- **Concrete Fix**: In `nadac_vs_mfp()`, match each weekly NADAC snapshot `as_of` against the MFP row active on that date (`MFP Effective Date <= as_of <= MFP End Date`, defaulting to `01/01/2026` for pre-2026 snapshots) so `Entresto Sprinkle` uses its active `06/01/2026` MFP after May 2026; report brand-classified (`Class == "B"`) ratios alongside all-NDC ratios for `NovoLog/Fiasp` (`3.99 -> 1.00`) and `Farxiga` (`3.17 -> 2.00`).
