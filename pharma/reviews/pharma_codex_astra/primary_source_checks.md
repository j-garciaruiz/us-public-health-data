# Primary-source checks, 2026-10-20

Companion to [report.md](report.md). This checks all nine paragraphs in “Things that trip people up,” plus the requested FUL, Big Four and California rules. Confirmed statements retain the scope below; interpretations are identified separately.

1. **NDC formats: confirmed for current 10-to-11-digit conversion.** The three padding examples are correct. The short segment must be known; an unsegmented 10-digit string is ambiguous. Keep identifiers as strings. FDA/openFDA use segmented product/package identifiers, while the NADAC and SDUD samples use 11 digits; the downloaded VA and IRA files use dashed 11-digit identifiers. Part D spending has names, not NDCs. RxNorm/name matching needs strength, form and ingredient validation and is not a unique crosswalk. Add the FDA's uniform 12-digit format, effective March 7, 2033, rather than treating today's formats as permanent. Sources: [FDA NDC format](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/electronic-drug-registration-and-listing-system-edrls/national-drug-code-format), [FDA NDC data](https://open.fda.gov/data/ndc/), [Utah Medicaid pharmacy manual, NDC conversion](https://medicaid.utah.gov/Documents/manuals/pdfs/Medicaid%20Provider%20Manuals/Pharmacy/Archive/2024/Pharmacy5-24.pdf), saved API/file checks.

2. **Product versus package: qualify.** “First 9 digits” is true only after normalization to the 11-digit 5-4-2 form. The package identifier is a key, not a guarantee that the monetary amount is a whole-package price. NADAC/FUL are per pricing unit. The downloaded IRA file contains NDC-9, NDC-11, a unit MFP and a 30-day-equivalent MFP; these are distinct denominators. Sources: [FDA NDC directory](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases/national-drug-code-directory), [CMS selected-drug file](https://www.cms.gov/files/zip/selected-drug-list-negotiated-prices-also-known-maximum-fair-prices-statutezip.zip), [inspected columns](supplementary_checks.json).

3. **List versus net: two material corrections.** WAC is a manufacturer list-price measure; NADAC estimates community-pharmacy invoice acquisition costs rather than patient payments or every pharmacy's realized cost. ASP already incorporates most rebates and concessions, with exclusions including Medicaid rebates. Therefore “none of these subtracts” payer rebates is false. Part D drug spending excludes manufacturer rebates, and SDUD reimbursement is pre-rebate and includes dispensing fees. Keep those dataset-specific statements. AMP and Best Price at manufacturer/product level are confidential; published weighted AMP in FUL is an important exception to a blanket assertion that no AMP-based values are public. CBO's historical findings support that net prices *can* be less than half of list, not a universal ratio. No adequate primary evidence was found for the categorical “nobody pays AWP” assertion or a universally applicable 20% markup; remove those absolutes. Sources: [ASP regulation, 42 CFR 414.804](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-414/subpart-J/section-414.804), [CMS Part D spending](https://data.cms.gov/summary-statistics-on-use-and-payments/medicare-medicaid-spending-by-drug/medicare-part-d-spending-by-drug), [SDUD field definitions](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/medicaid-state-drug-utilization-data-field-descriptions.pdf), [CBO price comparison](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/57007).

   **MFP is not exclusively a retrospective MTF refund.** CMS permits prospective access to MFP and retrospective refunds; manufacturers may use the MTF Payment Module or their own payment arrangements. A pharmacy can face a higher acquisition invoice followed by a refund, but that is one mechanism, not a required invoice pattern. NADAC alone cannot identify the net MFP effect. Sources: [CMS final guidance for IPAY 2027 and MFP effectuation in 2026–2027](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/medicare-drug-price-negotiation-final-guidance-ipay-2027-and-manufacturer-effectuation-mfp-2026-2027.pdf), [Federal Register MTF description](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-15/html/2025-06008.htm), [CMS dispensing-entity resources](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/medicare-prescription-drug-affordability/overview/medicare-drug-price-negotiation-program/pharmacy-dispensing-entity-resources).

4. **NADAC specifics: confirmed, with interpretation limits.** The program surveys monthly and publishes weekly; it targets retail community pharmacies rather than mail-order or specialty-pharmacy channels. This does not mean every specialty drug is excluded. All observed pharmacy indicators are C/I. The ten dates in the README are independently reproduced, but a majority changing Effective Date is a refresh proxy, not a causal attribution for each price change. EA, ML and GM are the observed units; the exact Ozempic 4 mg/3 mL tracer uses ML, so multiply its unit price by 3 for that pen's volume. Sources: [CMS retail price survey](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/prescription-drugs/retail-price-survey), [NADAC FAQ](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid-chip-program-information/by-topics/prescription-drugs/ful-nadac-downloads/nadacqa.pdf), [independent raw-data results](nadac_recompute.md).

5. **Suppression: counts confirmed, rule incomplete.** The live SDUD query returned exactly 652,561 suppressed rows out of 1,287,884 for 2026 Q1. SDUD suppresses low prescription counts and uses secondary suppression; `suppression_used=true` does not prove that a row has fewer than 11 prescriptions. Suppressed values must not become zero or enter a unit-price denominator. The Part D provider-drug file excludes combinations with 10 or fewer claims; age-65+ fields also have protective suppression. “CMS suppresses cells under 11” is too broad across unrelated products. Summing published SDUD amounts omits suppressed spending; “more in small states” was not quantified here and should be a hypothesis, not a result. Sources: [CMS SDUD suppression FAQ](https://www.medicaid.gov/faq/2020-04-09/91856), [current Part D prescriber methodology](https://data.cms.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/MUP_DPR_RY26_20260421_Methodology_508.pdf), [saved live count](live/sdud_suppression.json).

6. **Part D versus Part B: useful orientation, incorrect total.** Part B is not limited to facility-administered drugs or J-codes; specified oral cancer, transplant and other outpatient drugs are exceptions. HCPCS dosage units can differ from NADAC pricing units, so the crosswalk alone does not harmonize prices. The public Part B spending dataset covers traditional Medicare and excludes Medicare Advantage. Inpatient Part A drugs are also outside these two spending files. Replace “Medicare drug spending = Part D + Part B” with the actual covered populations and settings. Sources: [Medicare outpatient drug coverage](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/prescription-drugs-outpatient), [CMS Part B spending methodology](https://data.cms.gov/resources/medicare-part-b-spending-by-drug-methodology).

7. **FFS versus managed care: codes confirmed; causal claim unsupported.** FFSU and MCOU are the actual SDUD utilization types. Managed care expanded and pharmacy carve-outs matter, but enrollment arrangements do not establish that observed FFS drug trends are “mostly program design.” Examine state-year pharmacy benefits, enrollment and utilization before attributing changes. Sources: [SDUD definitions](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/medicaid-state-drug-utilization-data-field-descriptions.pdf), [MACPAC managed-care arrangements](https://www.macpac.gov/subtopic/types-of-managed-care-arrangements/).

8. **Drug-level versus person-level: confirmed.** Part D spending rows contain aggregate drug/manufacturer measures. MEPS prescribed-medicines files link to people and payment sources, with survey design and weights required for population inference; sparse individual-drug samples limit precision. Sources: [CMS API sample](live/cms_6_sample.json), [MEPS 2024 HC-254A documentation/download page](https://meps.ahrq.gov/mepsweb/data_stats/download_data_files_detail.jsp?cboPufNumber=HC-254A).

9. **Brand classifications: confirmed, not interchangeable.** NADAC B-ANDA/B-BIO, MDRP S/I/N categories and FDA therapeutic-equivalence classifications answer different questions. Define the intended classification and retain source labels. Sources: [NADAC comparison definitions](https://data.medicaid.gov/dataset/a217613c-12bc-5137-8b3a-ada0e4dad1ff), [CMS MDRP data](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/prescription-drugs/medicaid-drug-rebate-program/medicaid-drug-rebate-program-data), [FDA Orange Book](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases/orange-book-data-files).

10. **FUL: wrong if stated as an unconditional 175% formula.** The starting calculation is 175% of weighted average AMP, but CMS substitutes a higher percentage when necessary to meet the applicable average retail acquisition-cost floor, using NADAC. The live file contains an indicator for this adjustment. FUL is an aggregate federal payment limit for relevant multiple-source drugs, not a patient cash price. Source: [CMS Federal Upper Limit](https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/prescription-drugs/federal-upper-limit).

11. **Big Four: confirmed with scope.** The statutory federal ceiling for covered drugs is generally 76% of non-FAMP, with potential additional inflation discounts, equivalent to at least 24% below non-FAMP. It is not a universal discount from WAC, NADAC or every VA price line. Source: [VA OIG explanation of covered-drug pricing](https://vaoig.gov/sites/default/files/reports/2024-09/vaoig-24-01035-206.pdf).

12. **California: confirmed with precise window.** The increase-reporting rule applies above $40 WAC per course when the proposed increase plus increases in the current calendar year and previous two calendar years exceeds 16%, rather than an arbitrary rolling 36-month window. The separate new-drug rule uses the Medicare Part D specialty threshold. Sources: [HCAI increase reporting](https://hcai.ca.gov/visualizations/prescription-drug-cost-transparency-public-reporting/), [HCAI new-drug reporting](https://hcai.ca.gov/visualizations/drugs-introduced-to-market/).

Important missing cautions: FAERS reports can be duplicate or follow-up reports and cannot establish causality or incidence ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cder-conversations/understanding-cders-postmarket-safety-surveillance-programs-and-public-data)); an NDC listing does not establish FDA approval ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases/national-drug-code-directory)); NPPES registration does not establish licensure or an operating retail pharmacy ([CMS](https://download.cms.gov/nppes/NPI_Files.html)); current ARCOS reports omit buprenorphine for 2024 and 2025 pending review ([DEA](https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/arcos/retail_drug_summary/arcos-drug-summary-reports.html)).
