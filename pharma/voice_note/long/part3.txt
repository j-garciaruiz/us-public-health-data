Now the negotiation program. The Inflation Reduction Act, passed in twenty twenty-two, lets Medicare negotiate prices for some of its most expensive drugs for the first time. The negotiated price is called the Maximum Fair Price. A drug qualifies if it has no generic or biosimilar competitor, Medicare spends a lot on it, and enough time has passed since approval: seven years for pills, eleven years for biologic drugs. Drugs for rare diseases are mostly excluded, and a budget law in twenty twenty-five widened that exclusion.

The law sets a ceiling: the negotiated price must be at least twenty-five percent below the average price the company gets from non-government buyers, and at least sixty percent below for drugs that are sixteen years or more past approval, or lower still if the drug's real net price is already lower. Medicare can push below that ceiling. A company that refuses faces heavy penalties or can pull its products from Medicare and Medicaid, so all of them have signed.

How often? Every year, and each round takes about two years from selection to effect. Round one: ten drugs were picked in August twenty twenty-three, negotiated from February to August twenty twenty-four, the prices were published that month, and they took effect on January first, twenty twenty-six. So no, it did not start in twenty twenty-five; the negotiation happened in twenty twenty-four. Round two: fifteen drugs, negotiated during twenty twenty-five, prices published last November, in effect from January twenty twenty-seven. Round three: fifteen more, including the first drugs given in clinics, negotiated this year, prices out by the end of November, in effect in twenty twenty-eight. After that, up to twenty drugs a year.
