Finding three. Pharmacies still buy most of these drugs at well above the negotiated price. For Januvia, Xarelto, Entresto, Stelara and Enbrel, the pharmacy pays between two and three and a half times the negotiated price. The pharmacy hands the drug over, gets paid the low Medicare price, and then waits for a refund from the manufacturer. The government says refunds arrive in about twenty-one days on average. But in a survey by the independent pharmacy association, two thirds of owners said they wait more than twenty-two days, and six in ten are using their savings to stay afloat.

What does the literature say? We read eighteen papers and the key government and industry reports. Many studies predicted savings or looked at prices on paper. But we found nobody who has measured what actually happened to spending on all ten drugs in twenty twenty-six. The closest is one research team that looked only at Stelara. So our work adds something new, as long as we are careful with the word gross versus net.

And a link to your world. A government study from twenty twenty-four found that brand-name drugs cost about eleven times more in the United States than in Colombia, while generics cost about half. Colombia uses the United States as one of its reference countries for price ceilings. So these American list-price cuts could, in principle, travel into Colombian prices.
