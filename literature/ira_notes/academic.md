# Academic literature on the Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program (IRA): scoping scan

Status: scoping scan, 2026-10-20. Companion to `pharma/ira_findings.md` (our three findings: (1) Part D gross spending per claim fell about 64% (median) for the 10 drugs in 2026 Q1 vs 2025; (2) list-price (WAC) cuts on several selected drugs on 2026-01-01, and earlier cuts around the 2024 Medicaid rebate-cap removal; (3) NADAC stays 1.3–3.6x the MFP for most drugs).

## How searched

Searched on 2026-10-20 with the OpenAlex REST API (title/abstract and relevance search, 2018–2026, plus filters on the NBER and SSRN sources and on titles published since 2025-10-01), PubMed E-utilities (abstracts by DOI) and PMC full text where open, and WebSearch for coverage of studies whose text was not accessible. The Consensus MCP was tried once and its monthly quota was exhausted; the OpenAlex API hit its free daily budget near the end, so a few last searches (spillover to commercial prices, inflation rebates, stock-market event studies) did not run. Every paper listed was confirmed to exist through its OpenAlex record and/or its PubMed record with the DOI shown. "Cites" is the OpenAlex cited_by_count on 2026-10-20 (OpenAlex counts run well below Google Scholar). The "Source of claim" tag in each row says where the summary comes from: **A** = abstract, **FT** = full text (open access or PMC), **S** = only the one-line summary is available (research letter with no abstract and no accessible text), **P** = the paper's text was not accessible and the numbers come from press coverage of it (treat as unverified).

Peer-reviewed journal articles, NBER working papers and a few preprints (marked) are included. Health Affairs Forefront posts, ISPOR conference abstracts and grey literature are excluded (listed at the end).

## Gaps (what our data could add)

1. **Realized 2026 spending.** No published study (peer-reviewed or NBER/SSRN) measures what happened to Part D spending or prices for the 10 drugs after 2026-01-01; the literature has projections of net savings (Jeon 2026: −8.8% net), out-of-pocket models (Castle 2026) and formulary studies (Jain & McCoy 2026). Our finding 1 (gross per claim −64% median, comparison groups flat, fixed-weight) would be the first descriptive account of realized gross price changes, and the gross-vs-net contrast with Jeon et al. is a ready framing.
2. **List-price cuts timed to the MFP start date.** Nobody has documented that manufacturers cut WAC on Eliquis, Jardiance, Farxiga and Fiasp on 2026-01-01, nor the same-day cuts on non-selected sister brands (Boehringer Ingelheim's empagliflozin combinations, Tresiba, Lilly's Humalog U-200 and Lyumjev). The closest work is on the 2024 Medicaid cap removal (Levy 2024, Han 2026) and on launch prices (Shanmugam 2026, no IRA effect). A plausible mechanism to test (our hypothesis, not from the literature): under CMS's default refund (WAC minus MFP), cutting WAC toward the MFP shrinks refunds owed and pharmacy float, while also lowering Medicaid inflation-rebate exposure.
3. **Pharmacy acquisition cost vs MFP.** The only peer-reviewed treatment is a policy model for long-term-care pharmacies (Bhardwaj 2026, >85% revenue decline); refund-delay evidence is an association survey. Our NADAC/MFP ratios by NDC and date (finding 3) are an empirical, public-data measure of the benchmark gap no one has published, and could be extended to an exposure estimate with claim volumes.
4. **Deselection and generic/biosimilar overlap.** Four of ten MFPs end on 2026-12-31. Beinfeld 2025 models the Stelara overlap; no one has measured it. Our per-claim and claim-count changes for Entresto and Stelara, plus NADAC for the generics, could describe it.
5. **Pre-registration for the 2027 cohort.** Li 2026 and Gabriel 2026 give predicted and actual discounts for IPAY 2027; writing down expected per-claim and NADAC changes for those 15 drugs before 2027 data arrive would be a clean test that the literature has not done.
