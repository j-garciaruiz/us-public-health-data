# How others have used our data sources (NADAC, Part D Spending by Drug, MFP file, SDUD, Part D Prescriber, VA FSS, SSR Health)

Scan date: 2026-10-20. Context: `pharma/ira_findings.md` (first-year MFP study: Part D gross spending per claim, WAC-related NADAC changes, NADAC relative to MFP).

## How searched

On 2026-10-20 I read `pharma/ira_findings.md` and `pharma/README.md`, then searched OpenAlex (REST API and MCP; queries on NADAC, acquisition cost, spread pricing, Cost Plus, generic price spikes, Part D spending dashboard, per-unit prices, simulated negotiation, maximum fair price, Medicare vs VA prices, SDUD, rebate cap, SSR Health net prices, international price comparisons, US vs Latin America), Europe PMC (structured abstracts and Boolean searches on "National Average Drug Acquisition Cost", "State Drug Utilization Data", "Part D Spending"), and the web (KFF and Peterson-KFF, ASPE, CBO, GAO, OIG, 46brooklyn, NCPA, Colombian and Brazilian price regulators). Every row below was confirmed to exist by DOI (resolved through doi.org and OpenAlex) or by opening the URL. Data sources and numbers were taken from the abstract (Europe PMC or OpenAlex), the publisher full text, or the report PDF; where only secondary coverage was available this is said in the row. "Cites" is the OpenAlex `cited_by_count` retrieved on 2026-10-20 (grey literature has none). PMC article pages returned a browser check, so PMC text was read through NCBI E-utilities or Europe PMC instead; JAMA Internal Medicine, ACP and NEJM pages returned 403 for some items, noted where it matters.

Headline for positioning: I found no peer-reviewed or grey-literature study (as of 2026-10-20) that uses 2026 Quarterly Part D Spending by Drug data or 2026 NADAC files to look at what happened after the first MFPs took effect. Published work on the MFPs is either pre-implementation (simulations, price benchmarks, MFP vs net or international prices) or is monthly list-price tracking (46brooklyn) and pharmacy cash-flow reporting (Three Axis Advisors, NCPA). Our three-angle descriptive look at observed 2026 data appears to fill a real gap; the closest work is 46brooklyn's WAC tracking, which independently reports the same January 2026 list-price cuts.

## Methods lessons for our analysis

- Check MFP pass-through at the point of sale with the Part D plan pricing files (SPUF), as Hernandez et al. (2023) and Dusetzina (2024) did with formulary pricing files: plan unit prices by NDC for the ten drugs in 2026 would show directly whether plans price at the MFP, which our per-claim spending only implies.
- Keep "gross" honest by bracketing, not by guessing net: Feldman et al. (2021) give class-level Part D discount rates; Hernandez et al. (Health Affairs Forefront 2024) and Wouters et al. (2024) give drug-level MFP vs estimated net gaps (8% to 42%); Jeon et al. (2026) project an 8.8% net reduction. Set our −61% fixed-mix gross change beside these, labeled as different measures.
- Brand NADAC is close to WAC (about 1% below on average, Levy et al. 2017), so our NADAC/MFP ratio is in practice a list-to-MFP ratio for retail community pharmacies; adding VA FSS or Big 4 prices (public, used by Levy et al., Li et al. 2025, Clemans-Cope et al. 2023, Wing et al. 2026) would give a lower public bracket. KFF reports Big Four prices above the MFP for all ten drugs, worth confirming with FSS files.
- NADAC is retail-only and excludes specialty pharmacies (Dusetzina 2024), and its 2024 chain-reporting change shifted generic rates (OSU PolicyRx 2025). Before reading 2024–2026 NADAC moves as market price changes, screen brand NDCs for level shifts not explained by WAC-reason rows.
- Imbruvica and Stelara lacked NADAC in the 2023 data Three Axis used; we also have no Imbruvica match. Say so as a known gap of the benchmark, not of our join.
- Per-claim and per-prescription measures mix price with days supply and strength (Shin et al. 2023 is an example of a "price proxy" that is mostly mix). The annual Part D file's per-dosage-unit spending, 30-day equivalents (CMS, Wouters), or label-based per-year dosing (Rome et al. 2022) are the established ways to standardize.
- Insulin and the 2024 rebate-cap removal need their own treatment: the cap removal drove insulin list cuts (Feldman and Rome 2023; KFF 2024) and product switches (Levy et al. 2024, Flovent). Track NDC entries and exits, not only price changes.
- Designs for a causal follow-up already used on these data: stacked difference-in-differences with SDUD (Wing et al. 2026), segmented trends around entry (Dickson et al. 2023), a continuous exposure such as Medicare share of sales (Egilman et al. 2025), and MCO vs FFS splits in SDUD (Hernandez and Gellad 2020).
- Weighting: 46brooklyn weights WAC changes by prior-year Medicaid spending from SDUD; we report unweighted NDC shares. A spending-weighted version would make our finding 2 directly comparable with theirs.
- Pre-register the 2027 group, as Cousin et al. (2024) and Li et al. (2025) did with predictions before CMS announcements.
- For a Latin America extension, RAND/ASPE (2024) Table B.1 already gives US vs Colombia, Mexico and Chile ratios from MIDAS; Moye-Holz and Vogler (2021) give the PPP per DDD method for LAC procurement prices; Colombia's reference basket includes the US (Circular 03 de 2013).
