## 1. The program in one page

- **Law and timeline.** The Inflation Reduction Act (Pub. L. 117-169, August 2022) lets Medicare negotiate prices (Maximum Fair Prices, MFPs) for high-spend single-source drugs: eligibility starts 7 years after FDA approval for small molecules and 11 years after licensure for biologics, with prices applying about two years after selection (roughly 9 and 13 years). First 10 Part D drugs selected 2023-08-29, prices published 2024-08-15, **in effect 2026-01-01**. Second cohort of 15 Part D drugs: prices published 2025-11-25, effective 2027-01-01. Third cohort of 15 (first Part B drugs, plus Tradjenta renegotiated): selected 2026-01-27; final offers due 2026-09-30, acceptance by 2026-10-31, **prices published by 2026-11-30**, effective 2028-01-01. Full verified timeline in [grey.md](ira_notes/grey.md).
- **What CMS claims.** Against 2023 net spending, the first 10 MFPs would have saved 22% (about $6 billion). Against 2024 net spending, the 2027 cohort would have saved 44% (about $12 billion), or 36% ($8.5 billion) once Coverage Gap Discount Program discounts are counted. Wouters et al. 2024 (JAMA) argue the 22% overstates savings because manufacturers of negotiated drugs are exempt from the mandatory Part D manufacturer discounts.
- **The list is already shrinking.** Stelara, Entresto and Xarelto (generic or biosimilar entry, determined October 2025) and NovoLog/Fiasp (March 2026) leave the program on 2027-01-01; Xeljanz (2028 cohort) on 2029-01-01.
- **Legal status.** The Supreme Court declined to hear the manufacturers' challenges on 2026-05-18 (AstraZeneca, Novo Nordisk, Novartis, Bristol Myers Squibb, Janssen, Boehringer Ingelheim; [Duane Morris](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/supreme_court_declines_hear_challenges_inflation_reduction_act_medicare_drug_price_0526.html)). The D.C. Circuit decided Teva Pharmaceuticals USA v. Kennedy (No. 25-5425) on 2026-08-18 ([opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/cadc/25-5425/25-5425-2026-08-18.html), checked by Codex; [Duane Morris summary](https://www.duanemorris.com/alerts/dc_circuit_upholds_key_medicare_drug_pricing_rules_leaves_opening_challenge_aspects_0826.html)): it upheld CMS's grouping of same-ingredient products from one manufacturer into a single selected drug, and held ripe Teva's challenge to CMS's "bona fide marketing" test for generics, which governs how fast a brand leaves the program. AbbVie's Botox suit was filed in February 2026 (status not confirmed).
- **Political changes around it.** The 2025 reconciliation law broadened the orphan-drug exclusion from the 2028 cohort (CBO letter of 2025-10-20, read in full: $8.8 billion over 2025–2034, up from $4.9 billion after adding Darzalex, Keytruda and Opdivo; range $6.7 to $10.9 billion depending on how formulations are grouped). The administration's most-favored-nation deals set a $245 Medicare GLP-1 price, below the $274 semaglutide MFP, with a GLP-1 bridge outside Part D through 2027; the GLOBE Part B model exempts drugs with an MFP in effect. The "pill penalty" fix (EPIC Act) was not found to have passed. A proposed rule (CMS-4215-P, 2026-06-16) would codify the program.

## 2. How big are the price cuts, and against what

The single most important lesson for our study: **list, net and MFP are three different prices, and these drugs carried very large rebates.**

| Source | What it shows |
|---|---|
| MedPAC, March 2026, ch. 13 ([PDF](https://www.medpac.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Mar26_Ch13_MedPAC_Report_To_Congress_SEC.pdf), read in full) | In 2024, rebates reduced gross Part D costs by **nearly 45% for the 2026 cohort** and about 34% for the 2027 cohort (all single-source drugs: just under 30%). Rebates on the 2026 drugs totalled nearly $28 billion, about 35% of all Part D rebates. MedPAC concludes the program looks more effective for the 2027 cohort, which had less rebate competition. |
| Wouters et al. 2024, JAMA ([10.1001/jama.2024.22582](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.22582)) | IPAY 2026 MFPs were 8% (dapagliflozin) to 42% (sitagliptin) below estimated 2021 net prices (full text). Apart from insulin, every negotiated price stayed above list prices in six peer countries (Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Switzerland, UK), though the gap narrowed; mean ratios in their table run from 1.1 to 4.3. |
| Hernandez, Gabriel & Dickson 2023, JMCP ([10.18553/jmcp.2023.29.8.868](https://doi.org/10.18553/jmcp.2023.29.8.868)) | Among drugs then predicted for selection, the statutory minimum discount exceeded estimated 2020 rebates for etanercept, ibrutinib, palbociclib and enzalutamide; for the others, net prices would set the bar. |
| Sullivan et al. 2024, Value in Health ([10.1016/j.jval.2024.08.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jval.2024.08.001)) | Modelled CMS initial offers: ceiling prices likely bound for ibrutinib and ustekinumab; comparator net prices for the rest, etanercept included. |
| Jeon et al. 2026, Value in Health ([10.1016/j.jval.2026.03.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jval.2026.03.010)) | Projected 2026 Part D **net** spending on the 10 drugs: $26.1 billion without negotiation vs $23.8 billion with it, **−8.8%**. |
| Gabriel et al. 2026, JMCP ([10.18553/jmcp.2026.32.5.530](https://doi.org/10.18553/jmcp.2026.32.5.530)); Li et al. 2026, Value in Health ([10.1016/j.jval.2025.09.3054](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jval.2025.09.3054)) | IPAY 2027 MFPs: a median 38% below estimated net prices (range 15–60%), yet a median 108% above purchasing-power-adjusted prices in most-favored-nation countries. Li et al. had predicted CMS's initial offers (not final prices) at 32–78% off list. |

**What this means for our finding 1.** Our −64% median (−61% fixed-mix) drop is in gross Part D spending per claim, and these drugs carried rebates of about 45% of gross cost. Part of the gross drop therefore replaces rebates that were already being paid, so the net saving is smaller than the gross drop; how much smaller cannot be computed from these aggregates (the rebate shares are 2024 averages across drugs, our figures are per-claim medians, and 2026 rebates are unknown). Jeon et al. project −8.8% in net spending. Our headline must keep "gross" in the first sentence and set it beside the net projections rather than infer net savings from them.

## 3. What has been observed since 2026-01-01

**Realized spending: little published.** The scan found no paper or report measuring realized 2026 Part D spending across the ten drugs. The closest is Castle et al. 2026, JAMA Network Open ([10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.31556](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.31556), published 2026-08-17), which uses the 2026 Q1 Part D plan pricing file to describe prices and coverage of originator Stelara and its biosimilars (with projected annual spending). Other 2026 work covers different outcomes:

| Outcome | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Formulary access | Jain & McCoy 2026, Health Affairs Scholar ([10.1093/haschl/qxag290](https://doi.org/10.1093/haschl/qxag290)): every plan covers all 10 drugs in 2026; step therapy on negotiated diabetes drugs rose to 5.0% (comparators under 1%). Zheng et al. 2026 ([10.1093/haschl/qxag171](https://doi.org/10.1093/haschl/qxag171)): access to non-selected brands in competitive classes fell 11.8 points in stand-alone plans, 2024–2026. |
| Patient out-of-pocket | Castle et al. 2026, Annals ([10.7326/annals-26-02703](https://doi.org/10.7326/annals-26-02703)) (full text): using 2026 plan designs for beneficiaries without low-income subsidies, they model average annual out-of-pocket savings across six below-cap drugs of $685 in stand-alone plans and $470 in Medicare Advantage plans (reductions of 33–49% and 26–42%); in the model, users of Stelara, Imbruvica and Enbrel reach the $2,100 cap and get no additional direct savings. These are modelled, not observed, savings. Lederman et al. 2025, NBER ([10.3386/w33889](https://doi.org/10.3386/w33889)): with the 2025 cap and nonlinear cost sharing, most users see no change or a higher marginal OOP. |
| Benefit redesign (confounder) | Anderson et al. 2026, Health Affairs ([10.1377/hlthaff.2025.00644](https://doi.org/10.1377/hlthaff.2025.00644)); Zhang et al. 2026, AJHP ([10.1093/ajhp/zxag083](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajhp/zxag083)): the 2025 redesign raised deductibles and shifted tiers to coinsurance. |
| Pharmacy refunds | CMS fact sheet (April 2026, [PDF](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/pharmacy-dispensing-entity-mtf-fact-sheet.pdf), read in full): plans have 7 days to submit claim data and manufacturers 14 days to process; refunds reach pharmacies **within 21 days of the date of service, on average**. NCPA (2026-02-27, [notice](https://www.ncpa.org/newsroom/qam/2026/02/27/ncpa-shares-survey-results-cms-showing-mfp-refund-delays)): 67% of surveyed pharmacy owners waited 22 days or more from the date of service for refunds and 22% more than 28 days; 60% drew on pharmacy savings and 78% had trouble reconciling claims (letter to CMS of 2026-02-26, read in full: over 500 responses; [news release](https://ncpa.org/newsroom/news-releases/2026/02/27/ncpa-asks-cms-expedited-payments-and-other-corrections-mdpnp)). The American Society of Consultant Pharmacists and the Senior Care Pharmacy Coalition (survey release, 2025-12-30) report that 84% of responding long-term-care pharmacies planned to cut services and 78% to lay off staff; these are stated intentions, not measured outcomes. Long-term-care pharmacies: Bhardwaj et al. 2026, JMCP ([10.18553/jmcp.2026.25312](https://doi.org/10.18553/jmcp.2026.25312)) project that long-term-care pharmacy revenue net of acquisition cost on selected drugs falls by more than 85% in 2027 under their reimbursement and dispensing-fee assumptions (full text; a projection, not a measured 2026 outcome). |

**List-price cuts: documented in grey literature; no academic study of the 2026 cuts was identified in this scan.** NCPA's alert (dated 2025-12-10, [page](https://ncpa.org/wac-decreases-coming-take-action-now)) listed the coming WAC decreases: **Imbruvica 2025-12-22**; Eliquis, Farxiga, Jardiance, Glyxambi, Synjardy, Synjardy XR, Trijardy XR, Fiasp, Tresiba and Linzess on 2026-01-01; Lilly's Humalog U-200 and Lyumjev on 2026-02-01. 46brooklyn's January 2026 price review (Elsevier Gold Standard WAC data) reports Eliquis −43.0% and Jardiance −44.4%, the same figures as our NADAC rows. Drug Channels (2026-04-08) and a 46brooklyn year-in-review both count list-price cuts on **6 of the 10** products with a 2026 MFP (including cuts before 2026). Explanations offered (candidates, not established):

- Under CMS's default refund (WAC minus MFP), a lower WAC shrinks refunds owed and the amount pharmacies front. Drug Channels argues that list cuts toward net prices remove IRA-related pharmacy profits; total pharmacy margin also depends on acquisition terms and fees.
- Bristol Myers Squibb told Reuters (2026-02-05, syndicated copy read) that a lower Eliquis list price helps it avoid Medicare penalties for price increases above inflation. Under CMS's default refund (WAC minus MFP per eligible unit), a lower WAC also lowers the refund owed per unit.
- The cuts ended penny pricing in 340B. The Pennsylvania Office of Rural Health ([notice, 2026-06-26](https://www.porh.psu.edu/important-update-announced-about-340b-pricing-and-savings-on-five-common-drugs/), read) reports that from 2026-07-01 the 340B price rises to $245 a package for Eliquis, $113.83 for Farxiga, $120.05 for Jardiance and for Synjardy, and $166.38 for Linzess, because the January WAC cuts eliminated the inflation-related part of the 340B discount, which follows the Medicaid rebate formula (340B prices lag WAC changes by six months). This is a separate mechanism from the Medicare inflation penalty Bristol Myers Squibb cited. Both can offset part of what a manufacturer gives up with a lower list price, but their net effect on manufacturer revenue is not measured anywhere we found.
- Separately, Courtemanche 2026 (SSRN, [10.2139/ssrn.7615660](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.7615660)) calibrates the effect of the whole negotiation program, including reimbursement changes and commercial spillovers, on 340B covered entities: a projected net revenue loss of $5.73 billion in 2027, rising to over $24 billion a year within ten years under preferred assumptions.
- For insulin and some inhalers, the 2024 removal of the Medicaid rebate cap is the documented driver (KFF 2024; Levy et al. 2024, JAMA Health Forum, [10.1001/jamahealthforum.2024.3624](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2024.3624), on GSK replacing branded Flovent with an authorized generic rather than cutting the brand's price; Han et al. 2026, Health Affairs, [10.1377/hlthaff.2025.01309](https://doi.org/10.1377/hlthaff.2025.01309): generic share rose 23 points after the 2024 inhaler cuts).

## 4. Strategic responses and innovation

- **Launch prices.** Shanmugam et al. 2026, JAMA ([10.1001/jama.2026.17742](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.17742)) (full text): among 643 new drugs approved 2008–2025, launch prices rose about 7.8% a year before the IRA, with no significant change in level or trend after September 2022 (trend change −3.7% a year, 95% CI −23.6 to 21.4).
- **Trials and the "pill penalty".** Industry-funded studies report large drops: Schulthess et al. 2025 (Vital Transformation, Ther Innov Regul Sci, [10.1007/s43441-025-00773-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43441-025-00773-3), full text) find that, in the small-molecule subset of a selected cohort of 161 lead assets (83 small-molecule, 78 biologic), aggregate early-stage investment fell 68% from its peak before the IRA was introduced ($2 billion to $640 million by June 2024); this is a descriptive peak-to-2024 comparison, not an identified causal effect; other industry-associated studies are listed in [academic.md](ira_notes/academic.md); Kang et al. 2026, Health Affairs ([10.1377/hlthaff.2025.00720](https://doi.org/10.1377/hlthaff.2025.00720)) find overall trial activity stable, with fewer trials only at manufacturers with negotiated drugs. Wouters et al. 2024, JAMA ([10.1001/jama.2024.16911](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.16911)) find little evidence that biologics need the longer protection. Funding sources matter here and are recorded in [academic.md](ira_notes/academic.md).
- **Deselection.** Beinfeld et al. 2025 model the overlap of Stelara's MFP and biosimilars; Castle et al. 2026 (JAMA Network Open) describe 2026 prices and coverage during that overlap. Neither separates the effect of negotiation from that of biosimilar competition.

## 5. How others have used our data sources

| Source | Typical uses (examples in [data_uses.md](ira_notes/data_uses.md)) | Lessons for us |
|---|---|---|
| NADAC | Generic markups and PBM spread pricing (Medicaid, Part D), Cost Plus comparisons, generic price spikes; the closest IRA use is Three Axis Advisors for NCPA (January 2025), a pre-launch pharmacy cash-flow model built on 2023 NADAC, WAC (for drugs without a NADAC, Stelara and Imbruvica) and Part D utilization weights, assuming pharmacies pay wholesalers within about 10 days (full text) | In an April 2015 cross-section, brand NADAC averaged 99.1% of the minimum WAC across 1,436 brand drug groups (90.2% for 1,599 generic groups; Levy et al. 2018, Value in Health, full text), and CMS updates brand rates with WAC changes and CMS updates brand rates with WAC changes, so our NADAC/MFP ratio behaves like a list-related benchmark over MFP; it is not identical to WAC. NADAC excludes specialty pharmacies (Imbruvica and Stelara were missing in Three Axis's data too). Screen for level shifts from the 2024 chain-reporting change. |
| Part D Spending by Drug | Top-spending drugs, price growth per dosage unit, predicting which drugs get selected, savings simulations | Per-claim measures mix price with days supply; per-unit or 30-day-equivalent measures are standard. Use per-unit once the annual 2026 file exists. |
| MFP file | MFP vs list, net (SSR Health), VA and international prices (Wouters 2024; Gabriel 2026; KFF/Peterson: MFPs 2.8 times an 11-country average) | PORTAL (Harvard, 2024-12-13, [memo](https://eadn-wc03-8290287.nxedge.io/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/PORTAL_Price-Measures-for-UPLs-Memo_Final.pdf)) already compares NADAC, MFP and other price measures for selected drugs at one point in time. A weekly NDC-level NADAC-to-MFP series through 2026 was not identified. |
| SDUD and plan files | Medicaid insulin spending after list cuts; difference-in-differences around price events (Wing et al. 2026, NBER); Part D plan pricing files (SPUF) with NADAC (Hernandez et al. 2023; Dusetzina 2024) | The SPUF plan pricing file shows plan unit prices by NDC; matched on strength, supply and date it would indicate whether plans price the 10 drugs near the MFP (it does not show refunds or net spending). |

## 7. The Latin America angle

- RAND for ASPE (2024, [10.7249/rhq-11-03-05](https://doi.org/10.7249/rhq-11-03-05), 2022 IQVIA MIDAS data) gives US prices as a share of Colombian, Mexican and Chilean prices: Table B.1 (read in full): US prices as a percentage of Colombia's are 267% overall, 1,165% for brand-name originators and 48.5% for unbranded generics, and 779% for originators after a US net-price adjustment; Mexico 172/402/51 (311 net-adjusted), Chile 260/748/76 (500 net-adjusted).
- No comparison of MFPs with Latin American prices was identified in this scan.
- Colombia's Circular 03 de 2013 lists the United States among the 17 international reference countries; whether later circulars changed the basket, and which US price enters it, was not checked. If US list prices enter the reference, the January 2026 cuts could feed into Colombian price ceilings.
- Moye-Holz & Vogler 2021 ([10.1007/s40258-021-00670-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40258-021-00670-4)) give the PPP-per-DDD method for Latin American procurement prices.

## 8. Next steps the literature suggests for our study

1. Read the plan pricing files (SPUF 2026), matched on NDC, strength, supply and date, to check whether plans price the 10 drugs near the MFP (as Castle et al. 2026 did for Stelara).
2. Add a spending-weighted version of the WAC-cut share (46brooklyn weights by prior-year Medicaid spending from SDUD).
3. Bracket net with published estimates rather than guessing (MedPAC rebate shares, Wouters, Jeon); add VA Big Four prices as another public purchaser benchmark (KFF reports Big Four prices above the MFP for all ten drugs, so not a lower bound).
4. Write down expected 2027 changes for the 15 IPAY 2027 drugs now, with Gabriel and Li as benchmarks (the "write the outcomes down first" step in our findings).
5. Track the four deselected drugs after 2026-12-31 (Entresto and Stelara generics/biosimilars in NADAC and Part D).
6. Optional Latin America extension: MFP and NADAC against SISMED (Colombia) and CMED (Brazil) prices per DDD.
