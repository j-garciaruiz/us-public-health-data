## 3. Part B: Ranked Methodological Findings (8 Findings)

### Finding 1 (Severity: High) - `ira_findings.md` Misstates the October 2026 IPAY 2027 Median (`2.11x` vs. `2.5x`) and Overlooks Linzess's 2026 List-Price Cut and SPUF Row-Composition Shifts

- **Evidence:** In [`ratio_by_group.csv`](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/outputs/spuf/ratio_by_group.csv), the pooled median `UNIT_COST / MFP` ratio for the 15 IPAY 2027 drugs is `2.5245` (`307,519` rows) in `2025Q3` and `2.5254` (`289,411` rows) in `2025Q4`, but drops to **`2.1120`** (`273,546` rows) in `2026Q3`. Yet [`ira_findings.md` line 33](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/ira_findings.md#L33) claims they *"stayed at a median 2.5 times their future MFP in both 2026 releases"*, even though the table four lines below ([`ira_findings.md` line 42](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/ira_findings.md#L42)) prints `2.11x`.
- **Root Cause:** Two mechanisms lowered the pooled IPAY 2027 median between `2025Q4` and `2026Q3`:
  1. **Anticipatory WAC cut on Linzess:** As documented in Finding 2 of [`ira_findings.md`](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/ira_findings.md#L13-L16), AbbVie/Ironwood cut Linzess's list price by 50% on January 1, 2026 (`$17.93` to `$8.97` per capsule vs. `$4.53` 2027 MFP). That cut was not yet reflected in the January 5, 2026 SPUF build (`2025Q4` median ratio `3.9920` across `16,542` rows) but took effect by the October 2026 release (`2026Q3` median ratio `1.9860` across `16,488` rows).
  2. **SPUF row-composition shifts:** Between `2025Q4` and `2026Q3`, Vraylar (`1.9384x` MFP) gained two additional proxy NDCs (`+10,920` rows, from `22,056` to `32,976`), while higher-ratio drugs lost rows: Pomalyst (`2.6226x`, `-12,548` rows), Ofev (`2.0478x`, `-7,110` rows), and Janumet (`3.6343x`, `-2,998` rows), alongside Ibrance (`2.0017x`, `-11,100` rows). Across the 15 drug-level medians in [`ratio_by_drug.csv`](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/outputs/spuf/ratio_by_drug.csv), the unweighted median of the 15 drug medians is `2.8257` (`2025Q3`), `2.8035` (`2025Q4`), and `2.7011` (`2026Q3`).
- **Fix:** Update [`ira_findings.md` line 33](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/ira_findings.md#L33) to state that the 15 IPAY 2027 drugs had a pooled median of `2.53x` their future MFP in October 2025 and January 2026 and `2.11x` in October 2026 (with the shift from `2.53x` to `2.11x` driven by Linzess's 50% list-price cut on January 1, 2026 and SPUF formulary row-composition changes, and `0.0%` of rows at or below the 2027 MFP in any release).

### Finding 2 (Severity: High) - `ira_findings.md` Attributes All Above-MFP Prices in January 2026 to One Stelara NDC (`57894-0440-03`), Omitting Two Other NDCs and Failing to Distinguish Unbranded Ustekinumab From Branded Stelara

- **Evidence:** [`ira_findings.md` line 33](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/ira_findings.md#L33) states that *"The only prices above the MFP were on one Stelara product (45 mg/0.5 mL, NDC 57894-0440-03)"*. In fact, [`above_mfp_2026.csv`](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/outputs/spuf/above_mfp_2026.csv) and [`summary.md` lines 78-82](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/outputs/spuf/summary.md#L78-L82) show **three** NDCs above the MFP in `2025Q4` (`5,350` rows total):
  - `57894-0440-03` (45 mg/0.5 mL single-dose vial): `5,170 / 5,170` rows (`100.0%`) above MFP, median ratio `2.4706`, max `7.6998` (`96.64%` of all above-MFP rows).
  - `57894-0440-01` (45 mg/0.5 mL prefilled syringe): `138 / 4,662` rows (`2.96%`) above MFP, median ratio `1.1375`, max `2.5666` (`2.58%` of all above-MFP rows).
  - `57894-0441-01` (90 mg/1 mL prefilled syringe): `42 / 4,662` rows (`0.90%`) above MFP, median ratio `2.0275`, max `2.5665` (`0.79%` of all above-MFP rows).
- **Branded Stelara vs. Unbranded Ustekinumab:** Under CMS's IRA negotiation rules ([CMS IPAY 2026 Revised Final Guidance, Section 40.2](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/revised-medicare-drug-price-negotiation-program-guidance-initial-price-applicability-year-2026.pdf)), the negotiated MFP applies across all dosage forms, strengths, and package sizes of the selected drug marketed by the Primary Manufacturer under the NDA/BLA (BLA 125261). The 6 Stelara NDCs in SPUF split cleanly into two groups:
  1. **Branded Stelara (`57894-0060-02`, `57894-0060-03`, `57894-0061-03`):** `14,492` rows in `2025Q4` and `10,683` rows in `2026Q3`. **Zero rows (`0.00%`)** exceeded the MFP in either release (`99.98%` within 0.5% at `median = 1.0000`, `0.02%` below).

These three unbranded NDCs account for **100% of the 5,350 above-MFP rows** and **99.97% (`9,142 / 9,145`) of Stelara's below-MFP rows** in `2025Q4`.
- **Fix:** Revise [`ira_findings.md` line 33](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/ira_findings.md#L33) to state that branded Stelara (`57894-0060-*`, `57894-0061-*`) was `99.98%` at the MFP (`0%` above) in January 2026, and that all `5,350` above-MFP rows in January 2026 occurred on Janssen's three unbranded Ustekinumab NDCs: primarily `57894-0440-03` (`5,170` rows, `96.6%` of above-MFP rows, median `2.47x` MFP), plus `57894-0440-01` (`138` rows) and `57894-0441-01` (`42` rows), all of which were corrected to `0` rows above MFP by October 2026.

### Finding 3 (Severity: High) - Unweighted Plan-NDC Counting Over-Weights 6-NDC Specialty Drugs by 65x to 102x and Over-Weights MA-PDs Relative to PDPs

- **Evidence:** Counting raw `(plan, NDC)` rows weights each drug by `(number of plans covering) x (number of SPUF proxy NDCs)` and weights each plan equally regardless of beneficiary enrollment. Yet because they have only 2 to 6 SPUF proxy NDCs, they receive only **34.78%** of unweighted `2025Q4` rows and **31.40%** of unweighted `2026Q3` rows.
  - **Low-claim 6-NDC specialty drugs (`Stelara` and `Imbruvica`):**

### Finding 5 (Severity: Medium) - What SPUF `UNIT_COST` Includes and Excludes, and Why Finding 4's Concluding Sentence Slightly Overstates What SPUF Proves

- **What SPUF `UNIT_COST` Measures:** Per the CMS Prescription Drug Plan Formulary, Pharmacy Network, and Pricing Information Files documentation ([CMS SPUF Data Dictionaries and Methodology](https://www.cms.gov/data-research/files-for-order/non-identifiable-data-files/prescription-drug-plan-formulary-pharmacy-network-and-pricing-information-files)), `UNIT_COST` in the `pricing file` is the plan-reported **Medicare Plan Finder (MPF) negotiated retail drug ingredient price per unit** (averaged across in-network retail pharmacies in the plan/segment's price group for the specified `DAYS_SUPPLY`):
  1. **Excludes pharmacy dispensing fees:** Dispensing fees are reported separately in `pharmacy networks file PPUF_*.txt` as `BRAND_DISPENSING_FEE_30`, `GENERIC_DISPENSING_FEE_30`, and (new in CY 2026) `SELECTED_DISPENSING_FEE_30`.
  2. **Represents the total point-of-sale ingredient price before splitting out patient cost sharing:** Beneficiary copayments, coinsurance, and deductible rules are reported separately in `beneficiary cost file PPUF_*.txt` and `insulin beneficiary cost file PPUF_*.txt`. At the pharmacy counter, the total allowed ingredient charge (`UNIT_COST x quantity`) is divided between the beneficiary (cost sharing/deductible) and the plan.
  3. **Excludes post-point-of-sale rebates, DIR, and MTF retrospective manufacturer MFP refunds:** Under the IRA ([42 U.S.C. 1320f-2(a)(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title42-section1320f-2&num=0&edition=prelim) and [CMS IPAY 2026 Revised Final Guidance, Section 40.4](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/revised-medicare-drug-price-negotiation-program-guidance-initial-price-applicability-year-2026.pdf)), Part D plans and their PBMs must make the MFP available at the point of sale (and may negotiate lower prices). When a pharmacy's acquisition cost exceeds the MFP (because the manufacturer has not lowered WAC to the MFP), the manufacturer makes the pharmacy whole via a retrospective Standard Default Refund Amount (`WAC - MFP`) through the CMS Medicare Transaction Facilitator (MTF) within 14 days of claim adjudication. SPUF `UNIT_COST` captures the point-of-sale negotiated ingredient price ceiling (`MFP`), not the manufacturer-to-pharmacy MTF refund flow or any supplemental post-POS plan rebates.
  4. **Plan-submitted MPF schedule vs. realized PDE claims:** SPUF is the pricing schedule submitted by Part D sponsors to populate Medicare Plan Finder, rather than adjudicated Prescription Drug Event (PDE) claim records.
- **Assessment of Finding 4's Wording:** Most of Finding 4 in [`ira_findings.md`](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/ira_findings.md#L27-L36) is well supported once the two numerical errors in Disagreements 1 and 2 are fixed. However, the final sentence ([`ira_findings.md` line 35](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/ira_findings.md#L35)) states: *"This is the direct check that finding 1 lacked: the prices plans pay at the point of sale for these drugs are the negotiated prices."* That phrasing conflates the total point-of-sale ingredient price posted on Medicare Plan Finder (`UNIT_COST`, which is split between the plan and the beneficiary and excludes dispensing fees) with realized net plan payments on adjudicated PDE claims.
- **Fix:** Revise [`ira_findings.md` line 35](file:///Users/hari/Documents/VSC%20Python/US%20Data/.claude/worktrees/pharma-data-eae972/pharma/ira_findings.md#L35) to: *"This provides the Part D point-of-sale pricing check that Finding 1 lacked: in the Medicare Plan Finder pricing files submitted by Part D plans, the negotiated point-of-sale ingredient unit prices (before dispensing fees and beneficiary cost-sharing splits) match the statutory MFPs across all 10 drugs."*
