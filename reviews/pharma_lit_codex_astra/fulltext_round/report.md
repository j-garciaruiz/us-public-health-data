# Fact-check of the full-text-round edits

Checked 2026-10-20 against `diff.patch`, the supplied full texts, and the web sources identified below. **Corrections required.** Most newly supplied numbers match their sources. The material problems concern the population and interpretation of the Castle estimates, the NCPA delay threshold, and the conflation of Medicare inflation rebates with Medicaid/340B pricing mechanisms.

Scope: added or substantively changed assertions, including new verification/provenance assertions. Unchanged clauses carried inside replaced Markdown lines are not a new audit of the underlying data analysis. A CONFIRMED verdict means the cited source supports the assertion at the stated level, including an explicitly attributed forecast or survey response. It does not independently validate a paper's model, confidential prices, or the causal explanation offered by a company. WRONG identifies an incorrect value or materially incorrect scope/attribution. COULD NOT CHECK identifies evidence that does not establish the assertion.

Local evidence locators below refer to line numbers in `literature/ira_fulltext/text/`. Document locations refer to the current versions of `literature/ira_negotiation_literature_review.md` (LR) and `pharma/ira_findings.md` (Findings).

## Ranked fixes, eight maximum

1. **Separate the rebate programs and remove the unquantified claim that cuts are cheap.** LR:47 and Findings:25. Replace the conflation with: “BMS told Reuters that lowering Eliquis's list price helps avoid Medicare inflation penalties. Separately, PORH attributed higher 340B acquisition prices for five drugs to reduced inflation-related discounts after their WAC cuts. These mechanisms can offset part of a list-price reduction, but their net effect on manufacturer revenue is not measured here.” If discussing Medicaid, identify its rebate formula separately. Describe the Part D standard default refund as WAC minus MFP per eligible unit, rather than asserting measured reductions in total refunds.

2. **Restore Castle's modeled population and six-drug denominator.** LR:39. Use: “Using 2026 plan designs for beneficiaries without low-income subsidies, Castle et al. estimated average annual savings across six below-cap drugs of $685 in PDPs and $470 in MA-PDs, with reductions of 33–49% and 26–42%, respectively. The modeled comparisons showed no additional direct OOP savings for Stelara, Imbruvica or Enbrel.” The table does not establish realized patient savings.

3. **Keep Courtemanche's forecast separate from the end of penny pricing.** LR:47. The $5.73 billion and over-$24-billion figures are correctly transcribed, but their placement makes them easy to read as the cost of the five WAC cuts. Move them to a separate sentence or paragraph: “A separate calibration of the overall MDPNP, including reimbursement effects and commercial spillovers, projects net 340B revenue losses of $5.73 billion in 2027 and over $24 billion by 2036 under preferred assumptions.”

4. **Correct NCPA's threshold to 22 days or more.** LR:41. Preserve 67%, 22%, 60% and 78%. The attached survey and release resolve the cover letter's inconsistent wording. “Over 500 responses” is supported; “nearly 510 responses” is the attached survey's more precise description.

5. **Bound the Schulthess result by its sample and timing.** LR:53. Say “in the small-molecule subset of a selected 161-asset cohort,” or specify 83 small-molecule and 78 biologic assets. Name the broad investment measure rather than implying pure VC-round data, and note that the paper dates the policy introduction to September 2021. The reported 68% is a peak-to-June-2024 descriptive comparison, not an independently identified causal effect of August 2022 enactment.

6. **Define what Bhardwaj calls revenue and retain the projection year.** LR:41. Use “projects a reduction exceeding 85% in 2027 in selected-drug pharmacy revenue net of acquisition costs under its reimbursement and dispensing-fee assumptions.” The worked examples concern margins falling toward the dispensing fee. Do not describe this as an 85% loss of all LTC-pharmacy gross sales or a measured 2026 outcome.

7. **Specify Levy's benchmark and snapshot.** LR:60. Use: “In an April 2015 cross-section of 1,436 brand drug groups, NADAC averaged 99.1% of the minimum WAC within group; the ratio was 90.2% for 1,599 generic groups (Levy et al., 2018).” This supports a close price-level relationship in that sample. “Tracked” suggests a time-series test that this paper did not perform.

8. **Make the verification header describe the revised document accurately.** LR:3–5 and 91. The new full-text assertion now sits beside an unchanged sentence saying two papers' figures are still press-only, although Castle and Shanmugam have been upgraded. Also distinguish the prior review's “all fixes applied” statement from this new round, whose fixes remain unapplied. Teva can now be cited to the accessible full opinion as well as the summary. Historical access failures should remain dated history rather than current claims that a source cannot be checked.
