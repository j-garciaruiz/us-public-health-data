CDC's county estimates can be rebuilt from demographics

CDC PLACES models every county from age, sex, race and ethnicity, education, poverty and state. Feeding those same inputs back in recovers almost every map. A rate the Census measures directly is far less predictable.

Against measured outcomes, PLACES adds little

Deaths, Medicare diagnoses and Census disability: demographics and state predict each one better than PLACES does, and adding PLACES lifts accuracy by at most 2.3 points.

What PLACES is good for

Use it for

  • Estimating likely burden where no local data exist
  • Planning and needs assessments
  • Broad geographic patterns and state comparisons

Avoid it for

  • Finding counties that do better or worse than expected
  • Evaluating a local program or policy
  • Regressing PLACES on demographics or on other PLACES measures
  • Ranking similar counties against each other

Where a measured source exists for the same quantity, use it instead: death certificates, Medicare claims for older adults, or Census survey data.

How it was checked

Each analysis was rebuilt from raw data by an independent reviewer before any number reached this page. The reviews found real problems, and every one was fixed.

  1. Build

    Analysis written by Claude (Opus 5.5) under the author's direction, from public raw files.

  2. Replicate

    Codex (GPT-6 Astra) rebuilt every number from the raw data before reading the original code, then audited the code.

  3. Challenge

    Agy (Gemini 4 Argon) reviewed the method and recomputed the headline numbers. Its findings were verified before use.

  4. Fix

    Every finding was fixed or answered, and the fixes were verified again.

Problems the reviews caught

  • Pairwise correlations mixed different sets of counties
  • A headline covariance figure that was capped near 1.9% by construction
  • Zero-death counties kept in the data, against the CDC rule on counts of 9 or fewer
  • A renamed Alaska county dropped from the mortality data
  • Kentucky and Pennsylvania missing from every atlas layer
  • Negative expected death rates in four counties
  • A smoothing estimator missing its centering step
  • Chart wording that claimed more than the analysis showed

Method, data and limits

Data

  • CDC PLACES, 2025 county release (BRFSS 2022-2023)
  • CDC WONDER deaths 2018-2024, under the NCHS data use rules
  • CMS Mapping Medicare Disparities, 2023 claims
  • Census American Community Survey, 2019-2023

Method

  • Expected values: cross-fitted regression on county demographics, with an optional state term; Poisson regression for deaths
  • Deaths: standardized mortality ratios against national age-specific rates
  • Smoothing: empirical Bayes shrinkage using each county's sampling error

Limits

  • Measured is not the same as true disease: claims reflect diagnosis and coding, deaths reflect certificates
  • Connecticut has no mortality match; Medicare smoothing uses an approximate denominator
  • No test yet on other regions or years

Builds on Kong and Zhang, "The Use of Small Area Estimates in Place-Based Health Research", American Journal of Public Health, 2020.
